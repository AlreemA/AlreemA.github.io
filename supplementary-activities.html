A Reflection on the Nafis International Program with Huawei: Learning, Growth and Future Goals

Sometimes one opportunity can completely change the way you see your future.

This past summer I had the incredible opportunity to be part of the second cohort of the Nafis International Program with Huawei. The program took me from classrooms in Abu Dhabi to training centers and technology labs in China. I learned about AI, cybersecurity, cloud computing, big data and 5G, but the experience also helped me develop a global perspective, step out of my comfort zone, and see new pathways I could take to develop myself that I had never considered before.

Launched by the Emirati Talent Competitiveness Council and Huawei, the program gives selected UAE nationals the opportunity to gain international experience through technical training and professional exposure. Our first four weeks were based at Khalifa University in Abu Dhabi, followed by four weeks in China, mainly at Huawei’s training centers in Hangzhou and Shenzhen.

The first week’s lectures changed how I saw services I had taken for granted. We studied government cloud platforms, public services and emergency command centers. I use TAMM and rely on our police and emergency services without thinking about the systems behind them. E-government is as much an engineering achievement as it is a policy one. The second week, on 5G, optical networks and intelligent transportation, showed me how much depends on connectivity, and the complexity of the technology behind them.

The third week was my favorite. We worked through hands-on labs on prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), AI agents and model fine-tuning. Comparing DeepSeek and OpenAI made me curious about how politics and national priorities shape technology development in China and the West, and why some companies release their models openly while others keep them private. Our final week covered financial AI and cybersecurity for banking, plus sessions with leaders from key organizations.

After a rigorous selection process, I was fortunate to be chosen for the second phase of the program in China. I traveled to Hangzhou, where we spent our first two weeks, during which we also visited Shanghai and Beijing, before continuing our training in Shenzhen for the final two weeks.

The training in China built on what we had learned in Abu Dhabi. We covered big-data applications, large AI models, communications networks, 5G, cloud infrastructure, AI data centers and ransomware protection. Visiting Huawei’s labs and facilities helped me better understand the lectures. We saw data-center systems, wireless networks, storage technologies and cloud facilities, as well as flagship stores and exhibition halls. We also visited Huawei’s Ox Horn Campus in Dongguan. Seeing these places in person helped me understand the scale of the ICT infrastructure behind the technologies we had studied and its industry applications.

One of the most meaningful experiences of the program was our visit to the UAE Embassy in Beijing, where we met His Excellency Hussain bin Ibrahim Al Hammadi, the UAE Ambassador to China. His encouragement and guidance on pursuing higher education in China left a lasting impression on me. Hearing about the opportunities available to Emirati students, alongside experiencing life in China, opened my mind to pursuing a degree there. I left the visit motivated to explore this possibility, with a deeper appreciation for the support our country provides its students in pursuing opportunities abroad.

This was my first visit to China and I was relieved to find everyday life to be very safe and extremely convenient in the cities I visited, especially once I learned to use the local apps. Finding my way around, communicating across language barriers and handling unfamiliar situations really helped me become more confident and adaptable.

What impressed me most was the work ethic of the Chinese professionals we met. Their focus on diligence, commitment to excellence, and respect for time and punctuality made me reflect on my own habits. I want to bring that discipline to how I prepare for classes and manage my daily life.

I returned with greater ambition and a stronger sense of what I need to do next. Meeting talented people and seeing the environments they work in motivated me to take my own development more seriously. I want to become a better student, strengthen my technical skills so that I can compete and contribute alongside people of that caliber.

Since returning, I have started taking evening Chinese classes. My goal is to pass HSK 1 by the end of this year, followed by HSK 2 and HSK 3 by fall next year. So far, learning the language has been challenging but much easier than I expected, and it has been incredibly rewarding and exciting. It gives me a way to keep building on an experience that meant a great deal to me.

After graduating next year, I plan to pursue a master’s degree in China. My dream is to study Advanced Computing at Tsinghua University, but I will also apply to programs at other Chinesse universities.

I know these ambitions will require consistent effort and discipline. But my experience throughout the program gave me the confidence to pursue them and a clearer reason to work toward them. I am grateful for the opportunities that my country has given me throughout my studies, and I want to make good use of them: to learn as much as I can, challenge myself and bring that knowledge and experience back to serve the UAE.
